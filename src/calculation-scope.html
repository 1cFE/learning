Calculation and scope

These are hypothetical learning and demand assumptions, not predictions of fusion performance or measured market demand. Both designs use the same learning rate and fixed cost contribution. The rate applies only to costs above that contribution, not to the whole electricity cost.

New-order cost = fixed contribution + (first-unit cost − fixed contribution) × (1 − learning rate)log₂(completed units).

Due deliveries enter the experience count before that year's new orders are priced. Units still under construction do not add learning. Each cohort retains its order-time electricity cost. The chart shows the cost available to a new order, not the average cost of the existing fleet. Crossing a reference permits order growth; delivered units reflect the chosen lead time. Unit size and first-unit cost are independent inputs, without an assumed engineering relationship.

Limited early demand caps above-wholesale commitments, including the first unit and unfinished orders. Competitive orders can grow beyond that cap. The continuing-demand cases instead follow the article's early order schedules, scaled by the order-pace input. Faster growth after crossover assumes sufficient customers, factories and finance. The common production ceiling is expressed in GWe per year, not units per year.

Lifetime premium sums max(cohort cost − wholesale reference, 0) × cohort MWe × 8,760 hours/year × 90% × 30 years. It includes all cohorts delivered within the chart horizon, and their payments after it ends. Early customer premiums, procurement or subsidies may cover those payments. The sum is in constant dollars without discounting. It is neither upfront capital nor a measure of development or factory investment. Cheaper later cohorts do not cancel earlier commitments.

Completed production includes retired units, whose experience is assumed retained. Orders stop early enough for every modeled cohort to be delivered within the selected horizon. A qualifying new-order estimate near the endpoint may have no qualifying delivery modeled. Changing the unit size does not represent a mid-program redesign or assume transfer of learning to a different product.
