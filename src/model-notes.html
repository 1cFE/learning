Learning model assumptions

This is the v10 interactive calculation for the compact learning corridor post. It compares hypothetical deployment paths. It does not estimate the engineering cost or performance of a particular fusion design.

Cost and production

Each design starts with one completed first unit in year zero. New-order cost is the fixed cost contribution plus the first-unit cost above that contribution multiplied by (1 − learning rate)log₂(completed units). Both designs use the same learning rate. Experience counts completed units, including those that later retire. Units under construction do not count. Each ordered cohort retains its original electricity cost.

The starting compact unit is 10 MWe at $500/MWh, with a one-year delivery lead. The large unit is 1,000 MWe at $250/MWh, with a five-year lead. The default learning rate is 25% per production doubling above a $5/MWh fixed contribution. The wholesale reference is $60/MWh and the target is $10/MWh. These are chosen assumptions. The fixed contribution is not a validated engineering floor.

Early demand and crossover

The continuing compact path orders 2, 4, 8, 16, 24, 32, 40, 50, 60, 70, 80, 90, 100, 110, 120, 130, 140, 150, 160 and 170 units in years zero through 19, then adds ten units to each subsequent year's early order. The continuing large path orders one unit in year zero, two in year one and so on, up to ten per year. The order-pace input scales these two early schedules.

The limited compact path follows the compact schedule until it exhausts its early demand limit, initially 31 units including the first unit. The limited large path orders one unit every three years until its initial seven-unit limit is exhausted. These limits count all above-wholesale commitments, including unfinished orders. They describe demand that is willing to pay a premium or supported by procurement or subsidies.

Once a design's new-order cost reaches the wholesale reference, orders can exceed its early demand limit. Annual orders grow by 50% by default, rounded upward, subject to a common 25 GWe annual production ceiling. If there was an ordering gap, at least one competitive unit starts the expansion when capacity permits. The ceiling also applies before crossover. The expansion assumes enough customers, manufacturing capacity and finance.

Deliveries are counted before new orders are priced each year. Orders stop early enough for their deliveries to fall within the chosen horizon, initially 45 years. The table separates the year a qualifying cost is available from the year a cohort ordered at that cost enters service.

Premium commitments

Lifetime premium for each cohort is max(cohort cost − wholesale reference, 0) × capacity in MWe × 8,760 hours/year × 90% utilization × 30 years. The reported total covers every cohort delivered within the selected horizon, including its payments after the horizon ends. It uses constant dollars without discounting. It is not upfront capital or development and factory investment. Below-wholesale generation does not offset earlier premium commitments.

Source and verification

The source repository includes the build instructions and automated checks. The browser calculation is available as model.js, with field definitions and interactive rules. Its default trajectories and floor/rate sensitivities are checked against the independently implemented Python reference calculation. Automated tests compare annual orders, deliveries, costs, cohort premiums and crossover dates across 13 reference paths, then check edge cases and deterministic randomized invariants.

The default compact continuing path reaches a new-order cost below $10/MWh in year 41, with the first corresponding delivery in year 42. Its year-45 new-order cost is about $9.70/MWh. These are conditional model results, not forecasts.

The frozen article version is at learning.1cf.energy/v10/. Its dashboard and source snapshots are protected by a build-time checksum. The chart uses D3 v7.9.0 under its included license. The dashboard has no account, analytics, external script or data dependency.
